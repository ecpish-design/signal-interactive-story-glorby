About the Signal Mission

What is it?

GLORB // Signal Mission is an explicit-teaching interactive story that helps students notice changing signals in themselves and others, connect body and behaviour clues with Signal levels, and practise choosing responses that may help.

What it is for

It makes changes in signals easier to notice by teaching Low, Steady, Rising and Overload one at a time, then applying them to Glorb and Tommy.

Learning focus

Students notice body clues, emotions and behaviour, match strategies to Signal levels, and choose a response that may support de-escalation.

Teaching aid, not a real-life test

Completion shows supported performance inside this lesson. It does not prove that a student can independently identify or respond to every real-life situation.

Learning sequence

NOTICE → CONNECT → IDENTIFY → RESPOND

Low Signal

Energy is running low.

Steady Signal

Things are working as expected.

Rising Signal

Something is building.

Signal Overload

The system is overwhelmed.

Students practise

Noticing body clues; noticing emotions and behaviour in others; matching strategies to Signal levels; and choosing a response that may support de-escalation.

Explicit teaching

The Signal categories are explained before learners are asked to apply them. AERO recommends explaining, demonstrating and modelling new content before less-guided practice.

Segmentation

The story breaks learning into smaller scenes and decisions rather than presenting the whole task at once. This is intended to reduce unnecessary cognitive load.

Formative feedback

Wrong responses explain the distinction and support another attempt instead of only marking the answer wrong.

Accessible interaction

The interface uses labelled controls, keyboard access, visible feedback, read-aloud support and responsive layouts. WCAG 2.2 includes guidance for reflow, readable labels, keyboard access and target size.

Important

This activity teaches a simplified framework for noticing changing signals. It is not a clinical, behavioural or psychological assessment.

Use with in-person teaching

Pair the story with discussion, adult modelling, real examples and opportunities to practise outside the screen.

Signals are contextual

People may show Low, Steady, Rising or Overload differently. The examples in this lesson are teaching examples, not fixed rules.

Support is individual

Different strategies help different people. The activity gives examples rather than prescribing one correct support for everyone.
